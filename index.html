The Founder

Why This
Scholarship Exists

Richard L. Crosby III spent years as one of Ohio’s top criminal defense attorneys — recognized among the state’s Top 100, making partner at a prominent firm, and later running as a candidate for U.S. Representative for Ohio’s 2nd Congressional District. He argued for the rights and freedom of others across hundreds of cases.

Today, Richard channels that experience into building — through the Comma Foundation, his nonprofit advancing affordable housing, eviction prevention, and reentry services, and through ventures under the RC3 Group. The Richard L. Crosby Scholarship extends that same mission directly to students: supporting the sons and daughters of the officers who serve on the front lines of the justice system, and the young people whose families have lived through what’s on the other side of it.

We don’t see these as two separate worlds. A household built around service and a household that’s weathered its hardest chapter often arrive at the same understanding of responsibility, consequence, and community — and this scholarship is built on the belief that students from both backgrounds can stand together, not apart, in the work of changing the systems that shaped them.

Two experiences.
One belief in what comes next.

A student raised in a law enforcement household and a student who’s navigated their family’s hardest chapter rarely get asked to stand in the same room. This scholarship starts from the idea that both understand something about service, consequence, and responsibility that most eighteen-year-olds haven’t had to learn yet — and both deserve support stepping into what’s next.

Two Paths, One Scholarship

Who Can
Apply.

Applicants qualify through either of the following — not both are required.

Path — Service Families

Children of Law Enforcement

Open to high school seniors who are the son, daughter, or dependent of a current or former law enforcement officer.

Path — Building Forward

Family & Personal Resilience

Open to high school seniors whose family has experienced a parent’s incarceration, or who have personally navigated legal trouble and are now working toward their education and future.

Questions

Frequently
Asked.

Do I need to qualify for both paths to apply?

No. You only need to meet the criteria for one of the two paths — either a law enforcement family background, or a family or personal history involving incarceration.

How is the $1,000 paid out?

Funds are sent directly to the financial aid or bursar's office of the college or trade school you'll be attending — not to the student directly.

Can I apply if I'm attending a trade or vocational school instead of a four-year college?

Yes. Trade school and vocational program enrollment qualifies equally alongside four-year college or university enrollment.

Who do I contact with questions?

Email Richard@theRC3Group.com with any questions about eligibility or the application.
